## History

The Lenovo Thinkpad T430 laptop was released in 2012 at a price of around $1000 from what I could find. It had options for both Windows 7 and 8 at the time, and 2nd or 3rd gen (Sandy Bridge and Ivy Bridge) Intel Core CPUs, as well as an option for an NVIDIA Optimus GPU setup. It used DDR3 RAM, supporting up to 16GB, and had a mPCIe slot for a WWAN card.

## The Laptop

I bought my T430 used for $60 on ebay in the fall of 2019 as my previous laptop's screen hinge attachment points had been damaged when attempting to repair it. I decided to go with a used Thinkpad because it had the same type of CPU socket as my then-current laptop, so I'd be able to transfer over the more powerful i7-3630qm as well as the RAM and SATA SSD. Along with the laptop, I purchased a dock and beefy charger, as well as a new OEM battery, since the laptop didn't come with one. It arrived in good shape and worked great once I transferred the parts over to it.

It's worth noting that several of my friends at the time also had T430 laptops, which given that their machines had held up well, informed my decision to also purchase one.

## Hardware Upgrades

Around 2022, I began to dislike the low-resolution screen and found a way to upgrade it. One big problem was that the original screen used an LVDS connection, while newer LCD panels used an eDP connection. There was some discussion online about using a soecial adapter board to convert between the 2 connectors, and that there was a 14in panel from a Dell Alienware laptop that would fit, but I was skeptical. I ended up finding an LCD panel that had a slightly different model number but was supposedly compatible, and ended up purchasing the parts.

When the board and panel arrived, I pulled out a parts T430 that I had purchased not long before with a broken screen and connected it, and was elated to see that it worked great, including the brightness adjustment! After doing some light surgery on my daily-driver laptop, I had upgraded the screen, carefully tucking the converter board behind the LCD panel in the upper part of the laptop, something I would slightly regret doing later due to a bright spot that appeared in the screen later on.

It was a night-and-day difference going from a 1366x768 TN panel to a 1920x1080 IPS panel, despite the new panel having a glossty finish. That upgrade made that laptop usable for several more years.

At some point, I also tried to downgrade the keyboard by swapping in one from a T420 laptop, however many of the keys didn't work, and the BIOS update I tried to apply didn't appear to work properly.

## Retirement

Eventually though it got to the point where it was beginning to show its age. By the end of 2024, the Ivy Bridge CPU with all of the Spectre / Meltdown mitigations turned on was getting a little long in the tooth, and with the incoming administration it was clear that additional tariffs were going to be put into effect. While the machine was still perfectly usable, I figured it was time to upgrade before things became more expensive and ended up switching to a Framework 13 that weighs about half as much and seems about twice as powerful. As much as I liked my Thinkpad, carrying around a 5lb slab was getting a little old, and the new battery was beginning to show signs of wear again. However, I got about 5 years of use out of that laptop, and it didn't really start showing its age until it was 12 years old in 2024. I think that's pretty good for a laptop, and if Framework keeps their promise of building new parts for the Framework 13 chassis, then I should be able to get many more years of use out of my new laptop.

Honestly, I think the T430 was one of the last of its kind; a modular laptop that was designed to be repaired and to an extent upgraded, and the latest in a series of machines that had excellent linux hardware support. We've pivoted to a future where Lenovo's new Thinkpads don't have upgradable RAM, CPUs, or swappable batteries. Granted many of these restrictions are brought on by the hardware, especially the RAM where it needs to be soldered on in order to run at full speed, but there's also an overtone of planned obselesence that's going to land some of these machines in a landfill prematurely because they can't really be improved after the fact like many older machines can.
